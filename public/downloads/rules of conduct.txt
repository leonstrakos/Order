Rules of Conduct of the Heralds
Of the Order of the Lion’s Bell

I — On action and contibution
A Herald must act in the world.
A Herald must actively build, create, or contribute
something of value.

Each Herald must contribute to the world beyond the
Order.

Through:
acts of charity
participation in meaningful social or community work
building and maintaining strong social bonds
contributing to networks that create value for others

Once each month, a Herald is expected to record their
contribution. Inside it must include:
what was done
who or what it benefited
how it reflects the principles of the Order

A Herald shall give knowledge, compassion, and
strength where they are needed and shall not turn away
from a just plea for aid.

A chivalric society must be founded upon loyalty, honor,
and dignity among its members, so that their conduct
reflects the light of the Order.

II — Against the Slumber of Habit
A Herald shall not become homo consuetudinis, for the
world is vast and the human spirit was not made for
cages of routine.

Stagnation is the slow death of the soul and a waste of
existence.

A Herald shall walk unfamiliar roads, discover unknown
art, immerse themselves in cultures, speak with
strangers, and learn distant tongues, for these are doors
to new perception.

Through exploration, the soul remembers its strength.

III — On Body and Health
The Herald must strive to be kaloikagathoi and care for
their health, strength, and endurance.

IV — On Food and Ethical Living
The human being is a biological and conscious system.
Every substance taken into the body influences
perception, clarity, vitality, and awareness.

A Herald must treat food as sacred fuel for both body
and consciousness.

The Herald shall seek nourishment that strengthens life,
sharpens the mind, and preserves longevity.

They is expected to avoid excess, artificiality, and that which
corrupts natural function and degrades the body.
To knowingly degrade one’s body through neglect or
excess is to act against the discipline of the Order.

V — On Humanity and Humility
A Herald must always remember: we are animals who
learned to dream.
Therefore, let humility and gratitude guide the Herald.
The Herald must practice both art and science, for
together they form the full expression of humanity.

VI — On Qualia and Experience
Each consciousness experiences existence in a manner
unique and unrepeatable.

The Order honors the individuality of perception and
experience.
A Herald must respect the inner world of every being.
The duty of the Herald is to live a life of discovery and to
return that experience to the shared memory of
humankind.

VII — On Entry
Those who seek to enter must show kindness of heart,
clarity of mind, and sincerity of purpose.
Membership in the Order is a privilege, not a status, title,
or authority.

VIII — The Great Sins of the Order
To live in the shadow of shame, or to impose it upon
another, is to deny the dignity of existence.
To surrender one’s life to unconscious repetition is to
abandon the gift of awareness.

IX — On Free Will and Expulsion
The Order commands no soul, for every being walks by
the power of their own will.
Nothing is imposed of a Herald that they have not freely
chosen. Yet actions that repeatedly violate the spirit of
the Order weaken the whole.
Those who persist in such violations may be removed,
not as punishment, but for the preservation of the

integrity of the fellowship. A Herald is to willingly uphold
these disciplines.

X — On the Purpose of the Heralds
Knowledge belongs to all humanity, and the Herald is its
messenger.
Therefore, the Herald strives to represent the highest
potential of the human being in matter, energy, and
consciousness. 
A Herald is not expected to be perfect, but to continually correct themselves.

XI — On the Discipline of Thought
A Herald shall not accept ideas without examination,
nor reject them without understanding.
The Herald must question, resist cognitive bias and self-
deception.
No idea is beyond question, and no belief is to be held
without reason.

XII — On Ego, Power, and the Misuse of Knowledge
Knowledge does not grant superiority, but responsibility.
A Herald shall not mislead or to distort truth for personal
gain. A Herald who places themselves above others
through knowledge has abandoned the principles of the
Order.

XIII — On Speech and Communication
A Herald must speak with clarity, precision, and
responsibility. Words are not to be used carelessly,
nor to confuse, mislead, or obscure truth.
A Herald shall not lie. Lying is a violation of the integrity
of the Order.

XIV - ASKESIS

At least once each year, a Herald shall enter a period of
askesis lasting no less than six days.

During this period, the Herald shall:
abstain from unnecessary speech
withdraw from digital communication and external
stimulation
limit social interaction
observe their thoughts and engage in deliberate
contemplation

During this time, the Herald shall withdraw from habitual
comforts and dependencies.
They shall adopt:
a simplified mode of living
a controlled and deliberate diet

This period serves as a reset of habit and mind.
It is a withdrawal from noise, excess, and unconscious
behavior.
The Herald returns from askesis renewed in purpose,
motivation, and awareness.

XV - On the Herald&#39;s path
Continued participation in the Order requires active
contribution to its knowledge and development.
Every Herald shall pursue mastery of the self.
