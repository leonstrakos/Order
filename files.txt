# Hermetic Interpretation

Associated with the teachings attributed to Hermes Trismegistus.

Hermetic philosophy emphasizes:

* universal divine mind
* hidden knowledge revealed to the awakened
* truth present everywhere but rarely perceived

Your motto could be interpreted Hermetically as:

> **The call of awakening exists everywhere in the cosmos.
> Only those who become aware perceive it.**

The bell becomes a symbol of **gnosis** — the moment when hidden knowledge becomes audible.

It fits well with Hermetic ideas like:

> the divine speaks constantly through the world, but few recognize it.

---

# Pythagorean Interpretation

Connected to Pythagoras.

Pythagorean philosophy emphasizes:

* cosmic harmony
* the universe structured by number and proportion
* listening as a path to wisdom

Their followers were divided into groups, including the **akousmatikoi**, meaning:

> “those who listen.”

In that context your motto could mean:

> **The harmony of the cosmos calls to everyone.
> Wisdom belongs to those who listen.**

The bell becomes a metaphor for **cosmic harmony** — like the famous idea of the **music of the spheres**.

---

# Christian Interpretation

Associated with teachings attributed to Jesus Christ.

Christian scripture frequently uses **hearing** as a metaphor for spiritual awakening:

> “He who has ears to hear, let him hear.”

Your motto could be understood as:

> **The call to spiritual awakening is offered to all people.**

In Christian symbolism bells historically:

* call people to prayer
* mark sacred moments
* gather a community

So the bell ringing “for all” can symbolize **the universal invitation to salvation or truth**.

---

# Why It Works Across Traditions

Your phrase works because it expresses three universal ideas:

**1. Truth exists everywhere**
(Hermetic / metaphysical)

**2. Wisdom belongs to those who listen**
(Pythagorean / philosophical)

**3. Awakening is offered to everyone**
(Christian / spiritual)

Those themes appear repeatedly across ancient traditions.

---

# The Hidden Second Line

Even though you chose not to include it explicitly, your motto almost naturally suggests a silent continuation:

> **Campana sonat omnibus — sed non omnes audiunt.**

“The bell rings for all — but not all hear.”

That subtle tension between **universal truth and limited perception** is exactly what many mystical traditions emphasize.

---

# Overall Tone

Your motto feels:

* **Hermetic** in its idea of hidden knowledge
* **Pythagorean** in its emphasis on listening
* **Christian** in its universal call

But it doesn’t belong exclusively to any one of them, which actually makes it **very appropriate for a philosophical order that studies multiple traditions symbolically**.
